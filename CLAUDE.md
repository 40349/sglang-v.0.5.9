# CLAUDE.md

## Weekly Industry Project Slides（每週產學專案簡報規範）

Whenever preparing slides for the weekly industry project review, follow all of
the rules below. Source: `To_students.docx`, "Guidelines for Preparing Weekly
Industry Project Slides".

Additional standing rules for this project:

- Speaker notes (備忘稿) for English slides are written in Traditional Chinese.
- Before writing any report or slide, read the actual code in this repository.
  Never describe the implementation from memory.

### 1. Prepare Your Slides Carefully

- Treat every weekly presentation as a formal industry project review.
- Ensure your slides are clear, well-organized, and technically accurate.

### 2. Highlight Key Findings, Not Just Experimental Results

- Do not simply present tables, figures, or numerical results.
- Explain what you learned, why the results matter, and what conclusions can be drawn.
- Identify unexpected results, possible causes, and improvements.

### 3. Use Readable Slides

- Use a minimum font size of 18 pt throughout the presentation.
- Avoid overcrowded slides and excessive text.
- Prefer diagrams, charts, and concise bullet points.

### 4. Explain Research Papers Clearly

- Present the problem, key ideas, contributions, and overall system architecture.
- Use a concrete example to walk through the proposed approach step by step.
- Discuss the paper's strengths, limitations, and relevance to our project.

### 5. Demonstrate Weekly Research Progress

- Clearly distinguish completed work from ongoing tasks.
- Summarize major achievements, technical challenges, and lessons learned.
- Specify your next steps and planned experiments.

### 6. Use Meaningful and Informative Slide Titles

- Every slide must have a meaningful title that clearly communicates its main
  message or key finding.
- Avoid generic titles such as "Experimental Results," "Methodology," or "Progress."
- Use conclusion-oriented titles whenever possible. For example:
  - Instead of "Experimental Results," use "KV Cache Reuse Reduces TTFT by 25%."
  - Instead of "Model Comparison," use "Model A Achieves Higher Accuracy but Requires More Memory."
  - Instead of "Weekly Progress," use "Completed Cache Integration; Position Drift Remains a Challenge."
- Each slide should explain why, how, and what we learned, rather than merely
  listing activities.

### Pre-delivery checklist（交付前自我檢查）

- [ ] Every title states a conclusion or key finding (no generic titles).
- [ ] No text below 18 pt; no overcrowded slides.
- [ ] Every result slide explains what it means, why it matters, and any unexpected behavior.
- [ ] Paper slides cover problem → key idea → contributions → architecture → worked example → strengths / limitations / relevance.
- [ ] Progress slides separate Done vs. In progress, list challenges and lessons, and give next steps.
- [ ] Technical claims are checked against the code in this repo.
- [ ] Speaker notes are in Traditional Chinese.
